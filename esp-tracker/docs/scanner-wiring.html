Scanner Wiring

Pin-for-pin against scanner/include/pins.h. Power first, because that is what actually kills these builds — and the failure looks like a firmware bug for days.

Read before connecting anything

The SIM900 draws up to 2 A in transmit bursts at 3.4–4.4 V. It cannot run from the ESP32's 3V3 pin, from the DevKit's 5V pin, or straight off USB. Give it its own buck regulator to ~4 V, with a 1000–2200 µF electrolytic across its VCC/GND, physically close to the module, and short thick wires.

Without that capacitor the transmit burst collapses the rail, the ESP32 browns out and resets, and you get random reboots that look exactly like a firmware fault. Being mains powered removes the battery budget, not the current spike.

The RC522 is 3.3 V only. 5 V destroys it, permanently and immediately.

03

Level shifting GPIO 17

Only one line needs it. The SIM900's RX input is not 3.3 V tolerant, and driving it directly works on the bench for a while and then degrades the pin. A two-resistor divider is enough for a one-way 9600-baud line.

3b

The 4-pin RGB LED

Four pins means three colour legs and one common. Which kind you have decides the wiring and a firmware constant, and getting it wrong inverts every cue — the LED sits lit at idle and goes dark when something happens.

Symptom of picking the wrong one

Every LED lit at idle and dark during cues — the exact inverse of what you expect. The firmware is fine; flip LED_COMMON_ANODE. It is one constant because every write goes through a single helper in ui.cpp, which is what stops this becoming a hunt through a dozen call sites.

ColourMeans

Bring-up, one module at a time

Wiring all four then flashing the full firmware means any fault presents as "nothing works". Bring them up in this order — each step is independently verifiable.

01
ESP32 alone
Blink GPIO 26 and 27, sound GPIO 25. Confirms the board, the supply and your feedback wiring before anything else can confuse the picture.
02
DS3231 next, not last
Scan I²C and read the time. It is first because the scanner refuses taps without a trustworthy clock, so a missing RTC would otherwise look like a broken reader.
03
RC522
Dump a card UID over serial. If you get all-zeros or FFFFFFFF, it is wiring or a 5 V mistake — not the card.
04
SIM900 on its own supply — skippable
Before any code: power it, watch the status LED, then AT → OK, AT+CREG? → 0,1 or 0,5, AT+CSQ ≥ 10. Send one SMS by hand with AT+CMGF=1 and AT+CMGS.

Not fitted yet? Leave SIM900_PRESENT = false and skip to step 5. Taps are still read, stored and delivered over Wi-Fi, and the server texts the parent instead — nothing is lost and nothing is duplicated.
05
Full firmware
Only now. Serial should print scanner up | rtc=1 roster=N queued=0 sms=not fitted (server will send), or sms=ready once the modem is in.

Matches scanner/include/pins.h at the time of writing — if you change a pin, change it there first and regenerate this. GPIO 5 is a boot strapping pin (fine for an idle-high SS) and GPIO 12 is deliberately unused, since pulling it high at boot sets the flash voltage and bricks startup.
